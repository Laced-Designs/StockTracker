Options: price direction is only one input.

A call gives its holder the right to buy; a put gives the right to sell at a strike before or at expiry, depending on exercise style. Buyers pay a premium. Sellers take the corresponding obligation. Contract units depend on the product.

Learn the moving parts

InputWhat it changes
Strike and expiryThe price threshold and how long the right lasts.
Implied volatility (IV)The volatility consistent with a quoted premium under a pricing model. It is not a directional forecast.
Delta / gammaLocal sensitivity to the underlying price, and how delta changes.
Theta / vegaSensitivity to time passing and to changing implied volatility.
LiquidityBid/ask spread, executable size and open interest affect whether a theoretical edge can be traded.

A stock can rise while a call loses value if the move is too small, time passes, or implied volatility falls. A correct spot-price prediction does not establish an options edge.

What we need before testing options predictions
Point-in-time bid/ask chains, expirations and strikes, contract multipliers and exercise style, implied volatility surfaces, open interest, corporate actions and delisted contracts. Spot-price history cannot reconstruct those observations. The application does not invent historical option prices or claim that delta is a real-world win probability.
